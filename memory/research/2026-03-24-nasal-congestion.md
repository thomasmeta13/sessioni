# Research: Chronic Nasal Congestion — Root Causes
*Researched: 2026-03-24 by Scout*

## Summary
Chronic nasal congestion in a young adult is almost always one of four root causes: allergic rhinitis (most common, ~30% prevalence), chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS, inflammatory), structural causes (deviated septum, turbinate hypertrophy), or vasomotor/non-allergic rhinitis (autonomic dysregulation — most missed). The critical first question: does it respond to steroids/antihistamines (inflammatory/allergic) or not (structural/vasomotor)?

## Root Cause Taxonomy

| Category | Mechanism | Key Clue |
|---|---|---|
| Allergic rhinitis | IgE-mediated | Seasonal variation, itchy eyes, family atopy history |
| Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) | Mucosal inflammation | Facial pressure, reduced smell, nasal polyps |
| Structural | Deviated septum, turbinate hypertrophy | One-sided, no allergy symptoms |
| Vasomotor rhinitis | Autonomic/neurogenic | Triggered by temp, smells, alcohol, exercise — not allergens |

## Commonly Missed Diagnoses
- **Vasomotor rhinitis** — not on allergy panels, underdiagnosed. Triggers: cold air, strong smells, alcohol, spicy food. Treatment: ipratropium nasal spray.
- **Inferior turbinate hypertrophy** — bilateral, position-dependent. Needs endoscopy/CT. Responds poorly to meds, well to turbinate reduction.
- **Nasal valve collapse** — worse during deep breathing/exercise. Needs rhinomanometry to catch.
- **Silent GERD/LPR** — acid reflux without classic heartburn can cause congestion.
- **AERD/Samter's triad** — nasal polyps + asthma + aspirin sensitivity.

## Evidence-Based First Steps
1. **Trial intranasal corticosteroid** (Flonase/fluticasone OTC) — 4–8 weeks minimum. First-line per all major guidelines.
2. **Daily nasal saline irrigation** (NeilMed/Neti pot) — removes allergens, improves clearance, low risk.
3. **Add non-sedating antihistamine** (cetirizine/loratadine) if seasonal pattern.
4. **Track triggers** — 2-week log: symptoms vs environment, food, weather, alcohol.

## When to See an ENT
- Tried corticosteroids + antihistamines for 4–8 weeks → no improvement
- One-sided congestion (structural red flag)
- Reduced smell/taste (CRS or polyps)
- Recurrent sinus infections (>3/year)
- Years-long problem with no diagnosis → ENT workup worth doing anyway

## Diagnostic Pathway
1. Self-trial: corticosteroids + antihistamine + saline (4–8 weeks)
2. If no response → ENT: nasal endoscopy + CT sinuses
3. Allergy testing (skin prick or specific IgE blood test)
4. Consider rhinomanometry if structural cause suspected
