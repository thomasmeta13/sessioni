Repeatable Business Criteria

Every idea gets scored against this. If it doesn't pass, we don't build it.

Mechanical marketing
More ad spend = more revenue. Linear. Not about creativity — about volume. Turn the crank.
Proven demand
People already pay for this. We're not inventing a category. We're doing it better/cheaper/simpler.
15-second explainable
If you can't show it in a TikTok ad, you can't sell it mechanically.
Recurring revenue
Subscription > one-time. LTV needs to be 3x+ the cost to acquire.
2-week buildable
If it takes longer than 2 weeks to get to testable, it's too complex for this model.

The Models That Print Money

Noom$600M/yr
Model: Quiz → plan → $59/mo
CAC: $100-200
Audience: Women 25-55
What to steal: The quiz funnel. 10-question quiz = high engagement → personalized result = high conversion → subscription = recurring $. His ad skills map 1:1 to this.
Co-Star$15M/yr
Model: Daily AI content → $2.99/mo
Users: ~5M
Audience: Women 18-30
What to steal: Personality-driven daily content is crack for retention. AI generates it for free. The topic doesn't have to be astrology — could be anything personal and shareable.
Calm$300M/yr
Model: Content library → $14.99/mo
Audience: Adults 25-50
What to steal: Daily habit = retention. Sleep stories, meditations = content moat. But AI can make this 10x more personalized at 1/100th the content cost. No celebrity deals needed.
Hims / Hers$1B+/yr
Model: Quiz → consult → subscription products
CAC: ~$50-80
Audience: Women (Hers) + Men (Hims)
What to steal: Telehealth quiz funnel. Feels medical = trust = high willingness to pay. But we'd do digital product not physical — no pharmacy, no inventory.

Gaps We See

- Noom is $59/mo — there's room for a $9.99/mo version in a different vertical - Co-Star only does astrology — the daily personality AI model works for other topics - Calm's content is generic — AI personalization is barely used in wellness - Most quiz funnels sell to women 25-45 in health — other niches are wide open - Nobody is doing "quiz → AI companion" — combining the two highest-performing models
